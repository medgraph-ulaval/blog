# The Technological Healthcare Problem

When Jordan Mathieu and I set out to explore the concrete technological problems facing the healthcare industry in March 2026, we were eager and excited to contribute to a field centered on our humanistic values. The initial probing of the industry was done through dozens of empathy interviews with healthcare professionals, where we learned to grapple with their daily struggle. Those one-on-one moments were eye-opening for outsiders like us.

We were aware of some of the issues that plagued our current healthcare system (especially the one in the province of Quebec, Canada, where we originate), but the massive surprise was how deep the roots went.

We were not expecting to open the mother of all Pandora's boxes.

But we inadvertently did, and jumped right in.

## The Broken Tech Dream

As explained in *A Giant Leap* (2026), by Dr. Robert M. Wachter, the birth of Electronic Health Records (EHR) was initially enthusiastically welcomed as a liberation from unwieldy paper documentation.

But the early enthusiasm quickly died down when the complexity of building and maintaining digital data became apparent. Some opportunistic companies jumped on the occasion and gobbled up most of the market, while global incentives pushed towards siloed and closed data environments.

The results? A monopolistic technology environment where innovation has no place to grow and where clinics are captured, almost imprisoned, by enterprise systems that aren't always suited to their needs.

Health professionals weren't spared by the digital revolution either. The new information environment flooded their inbox and drained their time and energy.

The technological dream of a more efficient healthcare environment leading to better outcomes died, and we are still picking up the pieces.


## What comes next?

Countless courageous professionals are fighting on the frontline and accomplishing miracles every day. But that doesn't mean the status quo must remain unchanged. There is value in rethinking the data environment of the medical field.

Recent advancements in AI allow a more patient-centric ecosystem, empowering the patient while reducing the mental burden on clinicians.

Let's build this better future.
